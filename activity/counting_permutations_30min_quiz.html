02 · Quick review

Fundamental Counting Principle

If one event can happen in m ways and another event can happen in n ways, the two events can happen in:

m × n

04 · Quick review

Factorial notation

The exclamation mark means multiply a descending sequence of positive whole numbers.

5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120

Multiply

5! × 2! = 120 × 2 = 240

Add / subtract

6! − 4! = 720 − 24 = 696

Divide and cancel

10! / 5! = 10 × 9 × 8 × 7 × 6 = 30,240

Remember

0! = 1. Also, evaluate each factorial before adding or subtracting.

05 · Quick review

Permutations

A permutation is an arrangement where order matters.

All objects at once

ₙPₙ = n!

Arrange all 5 books: 5! = 120 ways.

Choose and arrange r objects

ₙPᵣ = n! / (n − r)!

Example: 5 cars, first 2 places: 5 × 4 = 20 ways.

President, secretary, and treasurer are different roles, so changing who holds a role creates a different arrangement.

08 · Detailed solution

Question 9: First and second place

Five race cars compete. We award first place and second place.

5 choices for 1st
×
4 choices for 2nd
=
20 ways
₅P₂ = 5! / (5 − 2)! = 5 × 4 = 20
Correct answer: C · 20

Why not 5 × 5? Once a car wins first place, it cannot also take second place. Only 4 cars remain.

The order matters: Car A first and Car B second is different from Car B first and Car A second.

1,320

12 × 11 × 10 = 1,320.

₁₂P₃ = 12! / (12 − 3)! = 1,320
Correct answer: D · 1,320

Why is this a permutation? Because the positions are different. A student being president is not the same as that student being treasurer.

10 · Key takeaway

How do I know which method to use?

Fundamental Counting Principle

Use multiplication when a task has multiple steps or stages.

Factorial

Use n! when arranging all n different objects.

Permutation

Use nPr when selecting and arranging only r objects from n, and order matters.

Remember: if order matters, think permutation.
